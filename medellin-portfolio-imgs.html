Site notes, design, and build

A semester of site analysis, design, and hands-on construction in Barrio La Cruz — documenting a community library project built alongside local residents as part of a study abroad practicum in urban planning and community engagement.

The project

The barrios of Medellin Colombia are often recognized for their intense terrain, and the comuna project held up as a symbol of social urbanism done right. Two decades of investment into communities previously overlooked because of their “informality” has led to drastic improvements in the quality of life for residents. However, not all benefits are felt equally among the comunas.

The women of La Cruz have long known that the children in their community faced insecurity that could lead them to gang recruitment. Roughly 10 years ago, several of these leaders came together to form Sueños de Papel, an after-school library program intended to give the children a space safe from these pressures. After renting and borrowing spaces for nearly a decade, they were ready to have a permanent library.

Site Analysis

Reading the area — the goal is to bring professional tools that highlight what the community tells us, not to tell them what they already know.

01

Property survey. The lot's official boundary survey, developed in AutoCAD as the base drawing for everything that followed.

02

Site basemap, cleaned up. Boundary lines, topography, and existing structures isolated so we can visualize our project.

03

Student pressure diagram. Visualizing the compounding pressures — insecurity, child labor, steep commutes — that force local kids out of school.

04

Sharing findings. Presenting the site analysis back to residents and partner staff, confirming priorities with them.

05

Community session. Working through site findings and data with our professors, notebooks and maps spread across the table.

06

Kickoff with our partners. The full team: students, Con-Vivamos staff, and Barrio La Cruz residents.

Design

Turning our findings into opportunities for intervention. This is all about aligning community priorities with brave ideas to maximize the design impact.

07

My first proposal, sketched. Tasked with mitigating the biggest risks highlighted by the community, I attempted to turn these interventions into opportunities for play and curiosity.

08

Park plan, simplified. My team took the CAD basemap and started overlaying our group design. We focused on terracing the slope, adding entry points, creating a retaining wall children could play on, and an outdoor stage with a collapsible sunroof.

09

Terraced drainage concept. An early proposal of mine which aimed to slow down and redirect the runoff from landslides and flashflooding. The idea was to integrate angular spillway blocks into a play-space for the children.

10

Site massing render. I developed our CAD file into a massing render in SketchUp to check scale and perspective.

11

Plaza rendering. An additional angle of the SketchUp render, focused on the stepped terrace and seating for the stage.

12

Potential model. This was used to present alternative garden placement options.

13

Materiality pass. Shifting focus to the building, this frame and roof design was decided on for its cost-effectiveness and availability of building materials. This model was also created in SketchUp.

Construction

Our days of construction were known as Convites. These were opportunities for collaboration between students, professionals, the library leaders, and any volunteers from the community.

14

Foundation week. The original floor of the site had been carved out by years of erosion and neglect. This massive foundation took days of terraforming and 9 batches of concrete mixed by hand.

15

Framing. Postholes were dug one meter into the ground to allow for 10 large steel columns to be placed. A hired welder then joined cross-beams on top, forming the frame for our roofing and walls.

16

Roofing goes up. Installing the corrugated thermoplastic panels over the finished block walls.

17

Accessibilty. A finished concrete stairway that allows safe access to each level of the yard.

18

The crew. Everyone who built this, in front of the mural — la biblioteca en la ladera.

19

Opening day. The finished library-park, and the first afternoon it was actually used. Concrete seating with arranged planter boxes, painted by the community groups who'll use this space.
